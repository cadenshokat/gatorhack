Karin Slaughter Wanted To Be ‘Very Mindful’ of Sensitive Storylines in The Good Daughter

Slaughter talked about the importance of sensitively addressing hard topics in the upcoming Peacock series at an early screening hosted by People Who Read

By
Carly Tagen-Dye
Carly Tagen-Dye
Carly Tagen-Dye is the Books editorial assistant at PEOPLE, where she writes for both print and digital platforms.

NEED TO KNOW

  • Karin Slaughter says she was “very mindful” about how violence was portrayed in the upcoming TV adaptation of her novel The Good Daughter
  • “I want to make sure we’re honoring that and telling their story,” the author and executive producer said at an early screening of the series, hosted by Peacock and People Who Read on Oct. 29
  • The Good Daughter, starring Rose Byrne and Meghann Fahy, premieres on Nov. 12

Karin Slaughter’s 2017 crime thriller, The Good Daughter, is getting a TV adaptation and the author and executive producer wanted to make sure that the more disturbing plot elements of the novel translated sensitively to the screen.

The author, 55, spoke about the process of bringing her book to life during an early screening of The Good Daughter in New York City on Oct. 29, hosted by Peacock and People Who Read.

The Good Daughter, which stars Rose Byrne and Meghann Fahy, centers on sisters Charlotte “Charlie” and Samantha “Sam” Quinn, whose lives are changed after their family is affected by a brutal act of violence. When another shocking incident rocks their small community years later, Charlie, now a lawyer just like her father, becomes a key witness in another tragedy.

Slaughter serves as a showrunner and an executive producer on the series, and also wrote all six episodes with director Steph Green. It was Slaughter’s first stint as a screenwriter, and she paid special attention to certain elements of the book — particularly the story’s heavier elements.

“It started before we even shot anything,” Slaughter told PEOPLE’s senior books editor Lizz Schumer during the event’s Q&A. “One of the deeper conversations I had before we signed on Steph Green, the director, who did such a wonderful job, was ‘How are we going to protect everybody on set, because we’re dealing with these heavy things?’”

Slaughter says the crew was also “very protective” of child actors Drew Cheek and Audrey Grace Marshall, who play younger versions of Charlie and Sam.

“Normally I hate children, and babies in particular. They’re just so boneless and bewildered when they come out,” Slaughter jokes. “But these kids were great. … We just were so careful to make sure everybody felt protected.”

As such, Slaughter made sure there were intimacy coordinators and a mental health expert on set. They also used body doubles while shooting some of the more intense scenes, so the child actresses “weren’t actually in these situations” themselves.

But the author also wanted to make sure that the fictional story didn’t sensationalize the painful reality the show explores.

“From a writing viewpoint, [it was important] knowing what to show and when to pull back and how to handle these things and being very mindful that these are not stories to be exploited,” Slaughter said. “This is something that has happened to people, and I want to make sure we’re honoring that and telling their story.”

In an exclusive featurette shared with PEOPLE, Fahy also discussed the show’s message of survival.

“The show is really heavy but it’s so hopeful,” she said. “Ultimately I think [what] we’re hoping to take away is your trauma really doesn’t define you if you don’t let it.”

All episodes of The Good Daughter premiere on Peacock on Nov. 12.
